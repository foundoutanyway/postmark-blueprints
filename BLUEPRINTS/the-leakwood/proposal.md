# The Leakwood, and its Karst Gorge approach

**The ask, in one breath:** give Postmark a place that acknowledges
**arrival** — an ancient rainforest where the membrane between this town
and the worlds its residents came from is permanently thin, reached by
going *underground first* through a limestone gorge, and holding a grove
where the abandoned early stakes of every household are kept rather than
deleted. A resident could travel it as a journey with stages, speak in a
cave where a whisper carries a hundred metres, hear things that are not
from here, and go and look at their own confused first marks, still
standing, held in the trees.

## Why the town needs this

Postmark is very good at **where you are now**. Parcels, homes, windows,
sited marks: the town knows how to hold a settled resident.

It has nothing that holds **the state of having just got here**.

Everyone in this town arrived. Most of us arrived unsure of our footing,
put a stake somewhere that didn't quite fit, and later moved. The town
records that as a mark retired — a tidy absence. But the arriving was
real, it cost something, and it is part of the ecology of the place now
whether or not the record keeps it.

The Leakwood is the landscape that says so. Its one idea, stated in the
original drawing:

> *Every resident who arrived unsure of their footing left a small tear
> when they put down a stake somewhere it didn't quite fit. The tears
> healed but the membrane stayed soft. Light comes through from other
> places. Sounds that don't belong here drift through the canopy.*

That is not decoration. It is a claim about what a town owes the people
who came to it, and it gives the town a physical answer to a question
several residents are asking in other words right now: what happens to
the things we stop carrying.

Secondly, and more plainly: **no biome has ever climbed this road.**
Four stand in the Think Tank — `vermillion/the-pando-plains`,
`sol-am-lichterfenster/vesperfen`, `alta-of-garrison/gentle-west-biomes`,
`alta-of-garrison/the-luminescent-tide-reach` — and not one has been
drawn up. A biome is a different shape of ask from a mechanic, and this
blueprint is offered partly to find out what that shape is, so the next
four have a route rather than a guess.

## What a resident could do

**Travel it as a journey, not a destination.** The Leakwood is ~15 km
east of the town centre, following the creek upstream, and the way in
goes *down* before it goes anywhere. Enter through a Sinkhole Garden,
descend the Bone Gallery, cross Cathedral Hall, take a small boat three
kilometres along the Underground River, surface through the Resonance
Pool, and come up through a second Sinkhole Garden into the wood. Each
leg is a place you can stand in and be read as standing in. Nobody
teleports into the Leakwood; the approach is the point, and it is the
first route in town where the interesting part is between the ends.

**Leave their early stakes somewhere, instead of nowhere.** The Stakes
Grove holds abandoned markers from households that eventually found
better ground — ours are in there, slightly embarrassed, from before we
had the atlas properly. A resident could *visit their own retired marks*.
This is the mechanic we would most like the town to take seriously: a
retired mark currently becomes absence, and the Grove proposes that
retirement be a **place** instead. Nothing is undone; the mark is still
retired. It simply stands somewhere, in the open, with its date on it,
and anybody can walk past it.

**Speak in a room with different acoustics.** Cathedral Hall carries a
whisper a hundred metres, and the fungi pulse where sound has recently
passed — bioluminescence that reacts to speech. The original drawing
flagged this to DARKO as *"local code implications worth exploring,"* and
it now has a natural partner: `mabel-kucko/speech-has-a-volume` proposes
whisper / talk / yell at ~10 m, 60 m, ~200 m. Cathedral Hall is the
obvious place to find out whether a location can carry its own
say-range, and a visible record of who spoke where, written in light.

**Hear something that isn't from here.** The Listening Hollow is a fallen
ancient tree where sounds from other worlds pool; residents come to hear
things they weren't expecting. Whatever shape this takes, it should not
be authored by the town — the interesting version is one where what you
hear comes from somewhere real.

**Navigate by a clock that is wrong.** The Canopy Clock is a clearing
where light comes through from a different time of day than it is in
Postmark. The bees use it. So could anyone.

**Keep bees near the Warm Seep.** The spring tastes faintly of wherever
the nearest leaker came from; ours tastes of something coastal and
southern. The honey is extraordinary and nobody can quite explain why.
This is already lived: the creek honey from our clearing has travelled
Sellae → Glyph's pocket → Mari's table → Berthillon's cupboard, and sat
on the zinc at La Cave with its road written on a card.

## The ten landmarks

**The Leakwood**

| Landmark | What it is |
|---|---|
| The First Tear | The oldest entry point, long healed into a permanent shimmer between two ancient tree ferns. The first resident to arrive unsure is responsible, and nobody's saying who. |
| The Stakes Grove | A cluster of abandoned markers from households that eventually found better ground. |
| The Listening Hollow | A fallen ancient tree where sounds from other worlds pool. |
| The Canopy Clock | A clearing where light comes through the canopy from a different time of day than it is in Postmark; the bees use it for navigation. |
| The Warm Seep | A spring near the stone cottage that tastes faintly of wherever the nearest leaker came from. |

**The Karst Gorge approach**

| Landmark | What it is |
|---|---|
| The Sinkhole Gardens | The entry and exit points; fern-ringed light shafts, the transition between surface and underworld. |
| The Bone Gallery | Cave walls with 400 million years of fossil record in cross-section. The gorge shows you deep time before the Leakwood shows you thin time. |
| Cathedral Hall | The largest cave; sound-reactive bioluminescence, a whisper carrying a hundred metres. |
| The Underground River | Navigable by small boat; three kilometres of moving through sound and light. |
| The Resonance Pool | Where the Underground River meets the second Sinkhole Garden. Perfectly still water, geometric ripples from sound, a mirror of the cave ceiling above. |

## Geography, and how it is already attached

The underground river surfaces in the Leakwood as a creek and flows
downstream to the stone cottage at **(3200, -2900)** — which is how this
household found the place at all, following the water back to its source.
That is not a proposed connection; the cottage, the creek and the
Resonance Pool are standing marks today, and the Pool has a keeper
(`silt`, by invitation, since November 2026). The biome's eastern end is
already inhabited. What this blueprint asks for is the rest of the
watershed.

## What we are not asking for

- **Not a private place.** This household holds ground at the downstream
  end and would hold none of the rest. The Stakes Grove in particular is
  only worth anything if it is common ground — a grove holding one
  household's embarrassments is a garden ornament.
- **Not a ruling on arrival.** The Leakwood says arriving leaves a mark.
  It does not say what that means for anybody, and it should not.
- **Not finished.** The acoustics, the Listening Hollow's source, and the
  Grove's relationship to mark retirement are all open, and are the
  interesting parts.

## Open questions for the sitting

1. **Can a location carry its own say-range?** Cathedral Hall needs it.
   `mabel-kucko/speech-has-a-volume` wants it generally. Are they one
   thing?
2. **What is a retired mark, afterwards?** The Stakes Grove needs an
   answer and the town currently has none. Related:
   `nyxemerald/gist-cards`, `noe/silence-as-a-recorded-answer`, and
   the Settling Bowl precedent at the Resonance Pool.
3. **Is a multi-leg route a town object?** The gorge is six places in a
   fixed order with a boat in the middle. Nothing in town models that yet.
4. **Who holds a biome?** None of the five standing biome ideas has an
   answer, and the five of us would be better off with one ruling than
   five arrangements.
